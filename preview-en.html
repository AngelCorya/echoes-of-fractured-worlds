Prologue — The Journey Begins

The alarm went off at five forty-five, exactly as it did every workday. Mark silenced it before the second chime—not out of discipline, but because his body had learned to anticipate the annoyance. For a few seconds he stared at the bedroom ceiling, where the orange streetlight drew a motionless band. In that brief interval, he could pretend the traffic, reheated coffee, and spreadsheets waiting at the office did not yet exist.

He got up, put on the blue shirt he had ironed the night before, and made coffee that was far too weak. As he drank, he checked his phone. Emma had sent a reminder about Friday's family dinner. Another message, sent by his father in the middle of the night, read: “I need to talk to you. Let's not put it off again.” Mark read it twice. He typed a reply, deleted it, and slipped the phone into his pocket.

The office occupied the seventh floor of a gray building surrounded by identical towers. The fluorescent lights stayed on even when sunlight poured through the windows, as though the company distrusted any illumination it could not control. Mark arrived at seven fifty-three. Sarah was already at her desk, reviewing three reports at once, while Thomas spoke to the coffee machine as if trying to persuade it to produce something drinkable.

“You're late,” Thomas said.

Mark glanced at the wall clock.

“There are seven minutes left,” Mark said.

“Exactly. A year ago you arrived with nine. The decline is obvious.”

Sarah did not look up from the documents. “Let him sit down before you diagnose him with a moral crisis,” she said.

“I'm only trying to keep the department's standards high.”

“Then start by turning in your report.”

Thomas looked at Mark with feigned gravity. “See how she treats me? At least your criticism is personalized. That's corporate affection.”

Mark barely smiled and turned on his computer. The same rhythm had repeated for years: Sarah demanded more than anyone considered reasonable; Thomas mocked anything that threatened to become too serious; and Mark stayed between them, solving problems before they became conversations. They were not friends, at least not in the sense he gave that word. They never met outside work and rarely spoke about their lives. Yet they knew the sound of one another's footsteps, the way each reacted to bad news, and the exact moment a joke stopped being a joke.

The morning passed between cost projections, last-minute corrections, and calls from a client who changed their mind with almost professional precision. Sarah left a folder beside Mark's keyboard.

“The boss wants this before lunch. The third-quarter numbers don't match the historical data,” Sarah said.

Mark flipped through the pages. “They combined two fiscal periods.”

Sarah stopped. “Had you already reviewed it?”

“No. It's in the first chart.”

Thomas leaned over from the next desk. “He doesn't need to review. He communicates with numbers in his dreams.”

“Numbers are clearer when nobody talks to them.”

Sarah hid a smile before returning to her desk. Mark corrected the report in less than an hour. When he sent it, she replied with a terse “received.” Five minutes later, a fresh cup of coffee appeared beside his hand. Sarah was already across the aisle and did not look back.

On Tuesday, Mark found Thomas waiting for the elevator with two bags of sweet bread. He offered one without explanation.

“What are we celebrating?” Mark asked.

“We survived Monday.”

“That happens every week.”

“Exactly. Consistency deserves recognition.”

They rode the elevator in silence. When they reached the seventh floor, the lights flickered and the panel briefly displayed a floor that did not exist: 0. Thomas stopped smiling. His hand landed on Mark's shoulder with a speed too precise, as though he expected an impact that never came. Then the indicator returned to seven and the doors opened.

“What was that?” Mark asked.

Thomas withdrew his hand. “A cheap building reminding us that we'll die here someday.”

Sarah was waiting near reception. She looked at the elevator panel, then at Thomas, and finally at Mark. “Are you all right?”

“Yes. The lights just went out.”

“Did you hear anything?”

The question was too specific. Mark was about to say no when he remembered a brief murmur, almost hidden beneath the hum of the motor. He had not understood the words, but he had felt someone pronounce his name from very far away.

“I'm not sure,” Mark said.

Sarah held his gaze for another second, then handed him a folder. “We have a meeting in ten minutes.”

The rest of the day seemed normal. Yet whenever Mark looked toward the corridor, he found Sarah watching him in the reflection of a window, or Thomas walking nearby for no apparent reason. It was not open surveillance. It was something harder to name—attention disguised as everyday habit.

On Wednesday, the team ate in a small room beside the archives. Thomas complained about the soup, Sarah checked emails while chewing, and Mark tried to finish a sandwich that tasted like a refrigerator.

“Have you ever thought about quitting and doing something completely irresponsible?” Thomas asked.

“You already practice irresponsibility full-time.”

“I mean something with purpose. Raise goats. Restore boats. Disappear into a country where nobody can ask me for a password.”

“You'd last two days before finding an office to criticize,” Mark said.

“What about you? Don't you ever want something different?”

The question came without its usual mocking tone. Mark looked at the plastic cup between his hands. “Sometimes,” he said.

Sarah set her phone on the table. “What?”

Mark took his time answering. He had no concrete image. He knew only the feeling of waiting for something that never arrived, as if his life had stopped before it began.

“I don't know. Something that doesn't feel the same every day.”

Thomas rested his elbows on the table. “Be careful asking the universe for change. It has a terrible sense of humor.”

Chapter I — The Living Forest

Mark stepped onto the path the pedestal had revealed, and a strange energy enveloped him completely. The transition felt like passing through a curtain of warm, luminous water. When he opened his eyes, he found himself in a place that defied all explanation: a lush forest where every leaf, branch, and breeze seemed alive, moving to the rhythm of a silent music. The colors were more vibrant than any he had ever seen, and the air was steeped in a sweet, penetrating fragrance that filled him with peace and bewilderment at once.

“Where am I? This can't be real… but it feels so vivid,” Mark said, looking around in disbelief.

He rose carefully, brushing away the leaves that had fallen on him. The talisman on his chest continued to glow with a steady blue light, and whenever he moved, he felt a deep connection to his surroundings. He took a few steps forward, listening closely to the sounds of the forest, which seemed to whisper incomprehensible words.

Suddenly, the crack of branches shattered the calm. Mark spun around, his heart racing. An imposing figure emerged from the mist. It was Lirian, the spirit of the forest. His body was formed from twisted branches and radiant leaves that seemed to flash with golden light, and his deep, shimmering eyes regarded Mark with a mixture of curiosity and judgment.

“Sel-viren-tha-lok, stranger. Your presence here disrupts the flow of life,” Lirian said in a deep, solemn voice.

“What…? Who are you? What is this place?” Mark asked, taking a step back.

The talisman on his chest began to shine more intensely, casting a faint blue light toward Lirian. The reaction seemed to calm the spirit, who inclined his head slightly before speaking again, this time in a language Mark could understand.

“Bearer… or should I say Herald. You are the first to cross into this world in centuries. Your arrival cannot be a coincidence,” Lirian said.

Before Mark could answer, hurried footsteps sounded and a figure emerged from the trees. It was Aelric, a young human warrior wearing clothes worn by the battles he had endured and carrying an improvised spear. He looked accustomed to the dangers of the forest.

“Don't hurt him, spirit! He is different. I can feel it,” Aelric said, interrupting.

“Can someone explain what is happening? I'm lost,” Mark said, looking at them both in confusion.

“That thing on your chest… it is not ordinary. Come with me, Herald. There are things you need to know. If you continue alone, this forest will devour you,” Aelric said, pointing to the talisman.

Lirian watched the exchange in silence, but a spark of approval showed in his eyes.

“The forest will rise with you if you prove worthy, Herald. Use the talisman wisely,” Lirian said.

Mark followed Aelric, who guided him along hidden paths between the trees. As they traveled, the young man explained what had happened to the Living Forest.

“This forest was once a place of balance, protected by a guardian named Kaelthar. But everything changed when he found a corrupted fragment of a talisman like yours. Ambition consumed him, and now he has become the Corruptor. His power feeds on the very essence of the forest,” Aelric said.

“Then… all of this is because of that corrupted talisman? How can I stop something like that?” Mark asked, trying to process the information.

“First, you must reach the Heart Tree, the center of the forest and the source of its energy. But it will not be easy. The Corruptor controls creatures that will not hesitate to destroy you.”

As they continued, Mark began to feel a deeper connection with the forest. The talisman responded to the world around him, glowing from time to time and allowing him to manipulate nearby roots and leaves to clear a path.

“This… this is different. It's as if the forest is trying to help me,” Mark murmured to himself.

“The forest is watching you, Herald. Perhaps it sees something in you that we cannot yet understand,” Aelric said with a smile.

With every step, Mark felt the weight of responsibility growing inside him. As he moved along the hidden trails, the talisman on his chest pulsed with a rhythmic glow, as though marking the way. The forest's silence was broken only by whispering leaves and Aelric's steady footsteps ahead.

“All of this seems so unreal. But if the talisman brought me here, there must be a purpose. Maybe I'm not just an ordinary man… or am I?” Mark thought.

The crack of nearby branches interrupted his thoughts, and both men stopped. A small creature emerged from the undergrowth, its body covered in moss and its eyes shining like emeralds. It seemed to study Mark with curiosity before giving a soft chirp.

“Don't worry. It's a Florian. They are the forest's messengers,” Aelric said, relaxing.

“A messenger? Is it bringing me a message?” Mark asked, crouching slightly to observe the creature.

The Florian approached timidly, dropped a small luminous sprout at Mark's feet, and then disappeared among the roots of a tree.

“The forest is testing you, Herald. That sprout is a guide. Hold it and you will see,” Aelric said with a smile.

Mark picked up the sprout, which released a warm light when it touched his skin. Visions of the Heart Tree and the Corruptor began to take shape in his mind: a dark figure wrapped in black roots, draining the life from the forest.

“So that is my objective… It seems I have no other choice,” Mark said gravely.

“You are not alone in this. The forest has allies who will appear when you need them most. But first, you must prove your worth,” Aelric said, placing a hand on Mark's shoulder.

With renewed determination, Mark continued onward, but his path was soon interrupted. Two deformed creatures emerged from between the trees, made of blackened roots and shadows that seemed to breathe hatred. They growled low and advanced slowly toward him.

“Prepare yourself, Herald! These are the Corruptor's beasts. They want to test your strength,” Aelric said, drawing his improvised spear.

Mark stepped back as the talisman began to shine more intensely. One of the creatures leaped at him and, acting on instinct, he raised his hands. Luminous roots burst from the ground and caught the beast in midair.

“What was that? I… I didn't know I could do that,” Mark said, gasping.

“The talisman is guiding you. Trust it, and trust yourself. Don't stop now,” Aelric said as he fought the other creature.

The second creature managed to knock Aelric down, but before it could attack, Mark felt a wave of energy surge through his body. He thrust his hands forward, and branches and leaves formed a shield before him, blocking the blow. He seized the opportunity to manipulate the roots beneath the creature and trap it as well.

“So I'm not fighting alone… the forest has chosen to trust me. I wish I could trust myself as easily,” Mark said with determination.

Both creatures dissolved into dark smoke, leaving a heavy silence behind them. Aelric rose with difficulty and looked at Mark with a mixture of surprise and respect.

“The forest responded to your intention, but Kaelthar will already have sensed the change. From now on, he will send creatures that know your limits,” Aelric said.

Mark nodded, wiping the sweat from his brow. He felt he had barely scratched the surface of what the talisman could do. Yet a spark of confidence was beginning to grow within him.

Before they could go any farther, a golden flash illuminated the path ahead. A tall, majestic figure emerged from among the trees, surrounded by an aura of warm light that seemed to permeate the entire forest. His features, as detailed as if nature itself had carved them, combined the strength of living wood with the softness of sap flowing beneath his skin. He carried an imposing staff, engraved with intricate patterns that pulsed with ancient energy, as though they told forgotten stories from ages past. It was Eryndor, Sage of the Forest, keeper of ancient knowledge and guardian of the Living Forest's memory.

“Herald, your footsteps in this forest are an echo of the past and a promise for the future. Before you continue, you must learn the truth of Kaelthar, whom they now call the Corruptor. Come, both of you,” Eryndor said in a calm, resonant voice.

With a gesture, Eryndor opened a path through the trees and led them to a hidden clearing, where a vast mural carved into the bark of an ancient tree told the history of the Living Forest. His fingers moved delicately, touching each symbol as he spoke.

“Kaelthar was once the guardian of this forest, a noble and wise protector. He understood every branch, every creature, every breeze. But when the dark fragment of the Primordial Talisman came into his hands, it slowly consumed him. It promised him power and absolute control, but in return it took his essence and corrupted it. His desire to protect the forest twisted into a longing to possess it, to shape it to his will,” Eryndor said.

“Why did no one stop him? Were there no other guardians?” Mark asked, looking at the mural.

“They tried, but the fragment gave him power none could match. Kaelthar turned the forest against us. Ancient trees became his weapons, and rivers that once gave life became barriers of destruction. In the end, we had to seal him away from the Heart Tree to keep him from destroying it completely. But the seal was not eternal.”

“And now you expect the Herald to do what no one else could. What is different this time?” Aelric asked skeptically.

“The talisman he carries is pure, a fragment untouched by darkness. But it is not only the talisman. The bearer is the one with the power to change destiny. Mark, your heart must be strong, for the Heart Tree will not reveal its secrets to just anyone,” Eryndor said, looking calmly at Aelric.

“I understand. But how do I use this power against Kaelthar? If he also has a fragment, how can I stop him without becoming like him?” Mark asked, taking a deep breath.

“That will be your greatest trial. The talisman amplifies not only your power, but your will. If you allow fear or ambition to cloud your judgment, you will follow Kaelthar's path. But if you act with purity and balance, the forest will guide you.”

The sage touched the center of the mural, and a golden light illuminated a route leading deep into the forest.

“This is your next step, Herald. Find the Heart Tree. Only there will you learn whether you are worthy to face the Corruptor,” Eryndor said.

At that moment, Mark's talisman began to glow with unusual intensity. His vision blurred, and the forest vanished, leaving him suspended in a vast darkness. An imposing figure emerged from the shadows before him. It was Malakar, a being encased in black armor that seemed to absorb the light around it. His red eyes burned like embers, and his voice resonated like distant thunder.

“So you are the Herald. How disappointing. I expected something more… impressive,” Malakar said mockingly.

“Who are you? What do you want from me?” Mark asked, trembling but resolute.

“I am Malakar, master of the dark fragments and sovereign of dimensions you cannot even imagine. The Corruptor—Kaelthar, as you call him—is nothing more than a pawn in my game. You, on the other hand, are a nuisance I intend to eliminate personally if you make it too far,” Malakar said with a laugh.

Mark clenched his fists, feeling the talisman struggle to repel the darkness radiating from Malakar.

“I'm not afraid of you. If Kaelthar is only a pawn, then I'll start by taking him down,” Mark said.

“Brave, but naive. You will soon discover that this game is not as simple as you believe, Herald. Your light is strong, but even the brightest light can be extinguished,” Malakar said with a cold smile.

The vision suddenly vanished, and Mark returned to the forest, falling to his knees as the talisman's glow faded. Eryndor watched him with concern.

“What happened, Herald? You look as though you saw something beyond this world,” Eryndor said, touching his shoulder.

“Malakar… that name thundered through my mind. He is more than an enemy; he is the dark force behind all of this. Kaelthar is only a piece on his board, the commander of an army stretching farther than I can imagine. This is much bigger than I thought,” Mark said, gasping, his voice unsteady.

“Malakar… That name has not been spoken in ages, and for good reason. Wherever his shadow falls, it leaves a trail of chaos and despair. If what you say is true, Herald, our struggle is not limited to Kaelthar. Malakar is a force that transcends dimensions, an architect of corruption who manipulates dark fragments to subjugate entire worlds. His ambition is insatiable, his reach infinite. This forest, vast and ancient though it is, is only a pawn on his eternal board. But even pawns have value in his game of destruction,” Eryndor said, his expression darkening as he lowered his gaze.

“But Eryndor, how do you know so much about him? Have you faced him before?” Mark pressed.

“Yes, master. If this Malakar is so dangerous, it seems you know more than you are telling us. What exactly happened?” Aelric asked, folding his arms and fixing his eyes on Eryndor.

“Very well… I will tell you what happened. But I must warn you: this memory is not easy to relive,” Eryndor said. He closed his eyes, and when he continued, his voice was lower and heavy with sorrow.

As he spoke, the air around them began to vibrate softly. A golden mist formed, and the trees seemed to darken as the past came alive before their eyes.

“Ages ago, my world was a bastion of balance and knowledge, protected by a network of guardians who maintained the flow of pure energy between dimensions. I was one of them. But Malakar arrived like a whisper in the night, slipping into the hearts of the most ambitious. With every dark fragment he distributed, he ignited internal conflicts, turned nations against one another, and finally plunged our world into chaos,” Eryndor said.

The vision revealed great cities burning beneath skies blackened by a massive shadow spreading like a plague.

“We tried to resist. We formed a coalition of the remaining guardians, but Malakar did not fight with brute force. He manipulated our minds and sowed betrayal. He made us destroy one another while he watched,” Eryndor said.

“How did you escape?” Mark whispered, horrified.

“I did not escape through courage, Herald. The forest saved me. When all was lost, the last pure fragments of my world sent me here. This place welcomed me and gave me refuge, but I cannot forget what I saw. Every battle, every betrayal… it was all part of his game,” Eryndor said, looking directly at Mark.

“Then how can we hope to defeat someone like that?” Aelric asked, clenching his fists.

“Because now we are not divided. But you must understand the nature of what you carry. The Primordial Talisman was broken into nine Dimensional Cores, each bound to one of the worlds that sustain the balance. Some cores remain whole; others were divided into seals or lesser fragments to prevent any single will from controlling them. Malakar does not possess separate talismans: he has created corrupted echoes, twisted imitations fueled by the Void. Your mission will be to restore the nine Cores and destroy or purify those echoes before he uses them to force the dimensions together,” Eryndor said with determination.

“Nine pure Cores? And what about the corrupted ones? How am I supposed to find and recover them?” Mark asked in astonishment.

“Each Dimensional Core is bound to a supreme guardian and, in certain worlds, protected by sentinels and lesser seals. Not all of them will allow you to approach easily. Some will test your strength; others, your compassion or your ability to renounce power. The corrupted echoes, however, will be held by those Malakar has broken. You must decide when to purify and when to destroy. No one else can make that choice for you,” Eryndor said with a grave nod.

“And how will we travel between dimensions? It doesn't seem as simple as walking from one forest to another,” Aelric said, folding his arms.

“The talisman is the key. With every Core you restore, its resonance will allow you to open portals to the connected dimensions. But be careful, Herald: every portal will take you closer to Malakar's influence, and his trials will become ever more demanding,” Eryndor said, raising his staff as it began to glow with an ethereal light.

“Part of me wants to stop now and find a way home. I don't know whether I have the strength or courage they expect from a Herald… but I cannot ignore what is happening either. So tell me: where is the first Core?” Mark asked, taking a deep breath.

“The first Core is here, in this forest. But do not be mistaken, Herald: even here, the path will be filled with challenges. You must prepare yourself, because what you face in this place will determine whether you are ready for what comes next,” Eryndor said, pointing toward the forest horizon.

Prologue — The Journey Begins

The alarm went off at five forty-five, exactly as it did every workday. Mark silenced it before the second chime—not out of discipline, but because his body had learned to anticipate the annoyance. For a few seconds he stared at the bedroom ceiling, where the orange streetlight drew a motionless band. In that brief interval, he could pretend the traffic, reheated coffee, and spreadsheets waiting at the office did not yet exist.

He got up, put on the blue shirt he had ironed the night before, and made coffee that was far too weak. As he drank, he checked his phone. Emma had sent a reminder about Friday's family dinner. Another message, sent by his father in the middle of the night, read: “I need to talk to you. Let's not put it off again.” Mark read it twice. He typed a reply, deleted it, and slipped the phone into his pocket.

The office occupied the seventh floor of a gray building surrounded by identical towers. The fluorescent lights stayed on even when sunlight poured through the windows, as though the company distrusted any illumination it could not control. Mark arrived at seven fifty-three. Sarah was already at her desk, reviewing three reports at once, while Thomas spoke to the coffee machine as if trying to persuade it to produce something drinkable.

“You're late,” Thomas said.

Mark glanced at the wall clock.

“There are seven minutes left,” Mark said.

“Exactly. A year ago you arrived with nine. The decline is obvious.”

Sarah did not look up from the documents. “Let him sit down before you diagnose him with a moral crisis,” she said.

“I'm only trying to keep the department's standards high.”

“Then start by turning in your report.”

Thomas looked at Mark with feigned gravity. “See how she treats me? At least your criticism is personalized. That's corporate affection.”

Mark barely smiled and turned on his computer. The same rhythm had repeated for years: Sarah demanded more than anyone considered reasonable; Thomas mocked anything that threatened to become too serious; and Mark stayed between them, solving problems before they became conversations. They were not friends, at least not in the sense he gave that word. They never met outside work and rarely spoke about their lives. Yet they knew the sound of one another's footsteps, the way each reacted to bad news, and the exact moment a joke stopped being a joke.

The morning passed between cost projections, last-minute corrections, and calls from a client who changed their mind with almost professional precision. Sarah left a folder beside Mark's keyboard.

“The boss wants this before lunch. The third-quarter numbers don't match the historical data,” Sarah said.

Mark flipped through the pages. “They combined two fiscal periods.”

Sarah stopped. “Had you already reviewed it?”

“No. It's in the first chart.”

Thomas leaned over from the next desk. “He doesn't need to review. He communicates with numbers in his dreams.”

“Numbers are clearer when nobody talks to them.”

Sarah hid a smile before returning to her desk. Mark corrected the report in less than an hour. When he sent it, she replied with a terse “received.” Five minutes later, a fresh cup of coffee appeared beside his hand. Sarah was already across the aisle and did not look back.

On Tuesday, Mark found Thomas waiting for the elevator with two bags of sweet bread. He offered one without explanation.

“What are we celebrating?” Mark asked.

“We survived Monday.”

“That happens every week.”

“Exactly. Consistency deserves recognition.”

They rode the elevator in silence. When they reached the seventh floor, the lights flickered and the panel briefly displayed a floor that did not exist: 0. Thomas stopped smiling. His hand landed on Mark's shoulder with a speed too precise, as though he expected an impact that never came. Then the indicator returned to seven and the doors opened.

“What was that?” Mark asked.

Thomas withdrew his hand. “A cheap building reminding us that we'll die here someday.”

Sarah was waiting near reception. She looked at the elevator panel, then at Thomas, and finally at Mark. “Are you all right?”

“Yes. The lights just went out.”

“Did you hear anything?”

The question was too specific. Mark was about to say no when he remembered a brief murmur, almost hidden beneath the hum of the motor. He had not understood the words, but he had felt someone pronounce his name from very far away.

“I'm not sure,” Mark said.

Sarah held his gaze for another second, then handed him a folder. “We have a meeting in ten minutes.”

The rest of the day seemed normal. Yet whenever Mark looked toward the corridor, he found Sarah watching him in the reflection of a window, or Thomas walking nearby for no apparent reason. It was not open surveillance. It was something harder to name—attention disguised as everyday habit.

On Wednesday, the team ate in a small room beside the archives. Thomas complained about the soup, Sarah checked emails while chewing, and Mark tried to finish a sandwich that tasted like a refrigerator.

“Have you ever thought about quitting and doing something completely irresponsible?” Thomas asked.

“You already practice irresponsibility full-time.”

“I mean something with purpose. Raise goats. Restore boats. Disappear into a country where nobody can ask me for a password.”

“You'd last two days before finding an office to criticize,” Mark said.

“What about you? Don't you ever want something different?”

The question came without its usual mocking tone. Mark looked at the plastic cup between his hands. “Sometimes,” he said.

Sarah set her phone on the table. “What?”

Mark took his time answering. He had no concrete image. He knew only the feeling of waiting for something that never arrived, as if his life had stopped before it began.

“I don't know. Something that doesn't feel the same every day.”

Thomas rested his elbows on the table. “Be careful asking the universe for change. It has a terrible sense of humor.”

Chapter I — The Living Forest

Mark took a step toward the path revealed by the pedestal, and an odd energy enveloped him completely. The transition felt like stepping through a warm, luminous curtain of water. When he opened his eyes, he found himself in a place that defied any explanation: a lush forest, where every leaf, branch, and breeze seemed alive, moving in time to a silent music. The colors were more vibrant than he’d ever seen, and the air was filled with a sweet, penetrating scent that brought both peace and confusion at once.

—Where am I? This can’t be real… but it feels so vivid —Mark said, looking around, incredulous.

He stood up carefully, shaking off the leaves that had fallen on him. In his chest, the talisman continued to glow with a steady blue light, and every time he moved, he felt a deep connection to the surrounding environment. He took a few steps forward, listening closely to the forest’s sounds, which seemed to whisper incomprehensible words.

Suddenly, the crackling of branches broke the stillness. Mark spun quickly, his heart racing. From among the mist, an imposing figure emerged. It was Lirian, the spirit of the forest. His body was formed of twisted branches and shining leaves that seemed to gleam with a golden light, and his eyes, deep and sparkling, observed Mark with a mixture of curiosity and judgment.

—Sel-viren-tha-lok, stranger. Your presence here disrupts the flow of life —Lirian said in a solemn, deep voice.

—What…? Who are you? What is this place? —Mark said, stepping back one pace.

The talisman in his chest began to glow more intensely, projecting a faint blue light toward Lirian. The spirit seemed to calm, tilting his head slightly before speaking again, this time in a language Mark could understand.

—Bearer… or rather, Herald. You are the first to cross into this world in centuries. Your arrival cannot be coincidence —Lirian said.

Before Mark could respond, the sound of hurried footsteps and a figure emerged from the trees. It was Aelric, a young human warrior with battle-worn clothes and a makeshift spear, clearly accustomed to the dangers of the forest.

—Don’t harm him, spirit! He’s different. I can feel it —Aelric said, interrupting.

—Can anyone explain what’s happening here? I’m lost —Mark said, looking between them in confusion.

—That thing in your chest… it’s not common. Come with me, Herald. There are things you need to know. If you go alone, this forest will devour you —Aelric said, pointing to the talisman.

Lirian watched the interaction in silence, but a spark of approval shone in his eyes.

—The forest will rise with you if you prove worthy, Herald. Use the talisman wisely —Lirian said.

Mark followed Aelric, who guided him through hidden paths between the trees. During the journey, the young human explained the nature of the Living Forest.

—This forest was a place of balance, protected by a guardian named Kaelthar. But everything changed when it found a corrupted fragment of a talisman similar to yours. Ambition consumed it, and now it has become the Corruptor. Its power feeds on the very essence of the forest —Aelric said.

—So… is all of this because of that corrupted talisman? How can I stop something like this? —Mark said, trying to process the information.

—First, you must reach the Heart Tree, the heart of the forest and the source of its energy. But it won’t be easy. The Corruptor has creatures under its control that won’t hesitate to destroy you.

As they advanced, Mark began to feel a deeper connection with the forest. The talisman responded to its environment, glowing occasionally and allowing him to manipulate nearby roots and leaves to clear a path.

—This… this is different. It’s like the forest is trying to help me —Mark murmured, to himself.

—The forest watches you, Herald. Perhaps it sees something in you that we haven’t yet been able to understand —Aelric said, smiling.

With every step, Mark felt the weight of responsibility growing inside him. As he moved through the hidden paths, the talisman on his chest pulsed with a rhythmic glow, as if marking the way. The silence of the forest was broken only by the whispering of leaves and the steady footsteps of Aelric beside him.

—Everything seems so unreal. But if the talisman brought me here, there must be a purpose. Maybe I’m not just an ordinary man… or maybe I am? —thought Mark.

The creak of nearby branches interrupted his thoughts, and both of them stopped. From the undergrowth emerged a small creature, with a body of moss and eyes bright as emeralds. It seemed to study Mark with curiosity before emitting a soft chirp.

—Don’t worry, it’s a Florián. They’re the forest’s messengers —Aelric said, relaxing.

—A messenger? Is it sending me a message? —Mark said, crouching slightly to observe the creature.

The Florián approached timidly, dropping a small glowing sprout at Mark’s feet before vanishing into the roots of a tree.

—The forest is testing you, Herald. That sprout is a guide. Hold it and you’ll see —Aelric said with a smile.

Mark picked up the sprout, which emitted a warm light upon touching his skin. In his mind, visions of the Heart Tree and the Corruptor began to take shape: a dark figure wrapped in black roots, draining life from the forest.

—So that’s my goal… It seems I have no other choice —Mark said in a serious tone.

—You’re not alone in this. The forest has allies that will appear when you need them most. But you must first prove your worth —Aelric said, placing a hand on Mark’s shoulder.

With renewed determination, Mark continued his path, but his steps didn’t last long before being interrupted. From among the trees, two deformed creatures emerged, made of blackened roots and shadows that seemed to breathe hatred. They emitted low growls and advanced slowly toward him.

—Get ready, Herald! These are the Corruptor’s beasts. They want to test your strength —Aelric said, drawing his makeshift lance.

Mark took a step back, feeling the talisman begin to glow with increasing intensity. One of the creatures leapt at him, and in an instinctive move, he raised his hands. Luminous roots erupted from the ground, snaring the beast mid-flight.

—What was that? I… I didn’t know I could do that —Mark said, gasping.

—The talisman is guiding you. Trust it and yourself. Don’t stop now —Aelric said, fighting off the other creature.

The second creature managed to knock Aelric down, but before it could attack, Mark felt a surge of energy ripple through his body. Extending his hands forward, branches and leaves formed a shield in front of him, blocking the strike. Seizing the moment, he manipulated the roots of the ground, immobilizing the second beast as well.

—So I’m not fighting alone… the forest has decided to trust me. I hope I can trust myself with the same ease —Mark said with determination.

Both creatures dissolved into a dark mist, leaving behind a heavy silence in their wake. Aelric rose with difficulty, looking at Mark with a mix of surprise and respect.

—The forest responded to your intent, but Kaelthar has already sensed the change. From now on, he will send creatures that know your limits —Aelric said.

Mark nodded, wiping sweat from his forehead. He felt he had only scratched the surface of what the talisman could do. Yet within him, a spark of confidence began to grow.

Before they could move further, a golden flash illuminated the path ahead. A tall, majestic figure emerged from among the trees, enveloped in a warm glow that seemed to saturate the entire environment. His features, detailed as if carved by nature itself, combined the firmness of living wood with the softness of sap flowing beneath his skin. He carried an imposing staff, intricately carved with patterns that pulsed with ancestral energy, as if telling forgotten stories from bygone eras. It was Eryndor, the Sage of the Forest, guardian of ancient knowledge and keeper of the Living Forest’s memory.

—Herald, your steps in this forest are an echo of the past and a promise of the future. Before you continue, you must know the truth about Kaelthar, now known as the Corruptor. Come, both of you —Eryndor said with a resonant, calm voice.

With a gesture, Eryndor opened a path through the trees, leading them to a hidden clearing where a massive mural carved into the bark of an ancient tree told the story of the Living Forest. His fingers moved with delicate precision, touching each symbol as he spoke.

—Kaelthar was once the guardian of this forest, a noble and wise protector. He understood every branch, every creature, every breeze. But when the dark fragment of the Primordial Talisman reached his hands, it slowly consumed him. It promised power, absolute control, but in exchange took his essence and corrupted it. His desire to protect the forest twisted into a hunger to possess it, to shape it to his will —Eryndor said.

—Why wasn’t he stopped? Were there no other guardians? —Mark said, looking at the mural.

—They tried, but the fragment gave him a power no one could match. Kaelthar used the forest against us. Ancient trees became his weapons, rivers that once brought life turned into barriers of destruction. In the end, we had to seal him away from the Heart Tree to prevent him from destroying it completely. But the seal was not eternal.

—And now you expect the Herald to do what no one else could. What’s different this time? —Aelric said with skepticism.

—The talisman he carries is pure, a fragment untouched by darkness. But it is not only the talisman. The bearer is the one with the power to change destiny. Mark, your heart must be strong, because the Heart Tree will not reveal its secrets to just anyone —Eryndor said, looking calmly at Aelric.

—I understand. But how do I use this power against Kaelthar? If he also has a fragment, how can I stop him without falling into what he did? —Mark said, breathing deeply.

—That will be your greatest trial. The talisman amplifies not only your power, but also your will. If you let fear or ambition cloud your judgment, you’ll follow the path of Kaelthar. But if you act with purity and balance, the forest will guide you.

The sage touched the center of the mural, and a golden light illuminated a path leading deep into the forest.

—This is your next step, Herald. Find the Heart Tree. Only there will you know if you are worthy of facing the Corruptor —Eryndor said.

At that moment, the talisman of Mark began to glow with unusual intensity. His vision blurred and the forest around him faded, leaving him floating in a vast darkness. Before him, an imposing figure emerged from the shadows. It was Malakar, a being wrapped in black armor that seemed to absorb light around it. His red eyes burned like coals, and his voice resonated like a distant thunderclap.

—So you are the Herald. Disappointing. I was expecting something more… impressive —Malakar said with a mocking tone.

—Who are you? What do you want from me? —Mark said, trembling but firm.

—I am Malakar, master of the dark fragments and sovereign of dimensions you cannot even imagine. The Corruptor, Kaelthar as you call him, is nothing more than a pawn in my game. You, on the other hand, are a nuisance I intend to eliminate personally if you get too far —Malakar said, laughing.

Mark clenched his fists, feeling the talisman trying to repel the darkness emanating from Malakar.

—I have no fear of you. If Kaelthar is just a pawn, then I’ll start by knocking him down —Mark said.

—Brave, but naive. You’ll soon discover this game isn’t as simple as you think, Herald. Your light is strong, but even the brightest light can be extinguished —Malakar said with a cold smile.

Suddenly, the vision faded and Mark returned to the forest, collapsing to his knees as the glow of the talisman dimmed. Eryndor watched him with concern.

—What happened, Herald? You seem to have seen something beyond this world —Eryndor said, touching his shoulder.

—Malakar… that name echoed in my mind. He’s not just an enemy. He’s a dark force behind all of this. Kaelthar is nothing more than a piece on his board, a commander of an army stretching beyond what I can imagine. This is far bigger than I thought —Mark said, gasping, voice broken.

—Malakar… That name hasn’t been spoken in ages, and for good reason. Wherever his shadow falls, it leaves behind a trail of chaos and despair. If what you’re saying is true, Herald, our struggle doesn’t just extend to Kaelthar. Malakar is a force that transcends dimensions, an architect of corruption who manipulates dark fragments to subjugate entire worlds. His ambition is insatiable, his reach infinite. This forest, though vast and ancient, is merely a pawn in his eternal game. But even pawns have value in his game of destruction —Eryndor said, his face grim, lowering his gaze.

—But, Eryndor, how do you know so much about him? Did you face him before? —Mark said, pressing onward.

—Yes, master. If this Malakar is truly that dangerous, it seems you know more than you’re letting on. What exactly happened? —Aelric said, crossing his arms, his eyes fixed on Eryndor.

—Very well… I’ll tell you what happened. But I must warn you that this memory isn’t easy to relive —Eryndor said. He closed his eyes; when he spoke again, his voice was lower, laced with melancholy.

As he spoke, the air around him began to vibrate gently. A golden mist formed at his side, and the trees seemed to darken as the past came alive before his eyes.

—Eons ago, my world was a bastion of balance and knowledge, protected by a network of guardians who maintained the flow of pure energy between dimensions. I was one of them. But Malakar arrived like a whisper in the night, infiltrating the hearts of the most ambitious. With every dark fragment he distributed, he ignited internal conflicts, pitted nations against each other, and ultimately plunged our world into chaos —Eryndor said.

The vision showed great cities burning, and skies darkened by a massive shadow spreading like a plague.

—We tried to resist. We formed a coalition of the remaining guardians, but Malakar didn’t fight with brute force. He manipulated our minds, sowed betrayal. He made us destroy each other, while he watched —Eryndor said.

—How did you escape? —Mark murmured, horrified.

—I didn’t do it out of courage, Herald. The forest saved me. When everything was lost, the last pure fragments of my world sent me here. This place took me in and gave me refuge, but I can’t forget what I saw. Every battle, every betrayal… it was all part of its game —Eryndor said, looking directly at Mark.

—So how do we hope to defeat someone like that? —Aelric said, clenching his fists.

—Because we’re no longer divided. But you must understand the nature of what you carry. The Primordial Talisman was shattered into nine Dimensional Cores, one linked to each world that sustains the balance. Some cores remain whole; others were divided into seals or smaller fragments to prevent a single will from dominating them. Malakar does not possess distinct talismans: he has created corrupted echoes, deformed imitations fed by the Void. Your mission will be to restore the nine cores and destroy or purify those echoes before he uses them to forcibly unite the dimensions —Eryndor said with determination.

—Nine pure cores? And the corrupted ones? How are we supposed to find them and recover them? —Mark said, surprised.

—Each Dimensional Core is linked to a supreme guardian and, in certain worlds, protected by watchers and minor seals. Not all will allow you easy access. Some will test your strength; others, your compassion or your ability to renounce power. The corrupted echoes, on the other hand, will be in the hands of those Malakar has broken. You will have to decide when to purify and when to destroy. That choice no one else can make for you —Eryndor said, nodding with gravity.

—And how will we move between dimensions? It doesn’t seem that simple as walking from one forest to another —Aelric said, crossing his arms.

—The talisman is the key. With each core you restore, its resonance will allow you to open portals to the linked dimensions. But be careful, Herald: every portal will bring you closer to the influence of Malakar, and his trials will become increasingly demanding —Eryndor said, raising his staff, which began to glow with an ethereal light.

—A part of me wants to stop right now and find a way back home. I don’t know if I have the strength or the courage they expect from a Herald… but I can’t ignore what’s happening. So tell me: where is the first Dimensional Core? —Mark said, breathing deeply.

—The first Dimensional Core is here, in this forest. But don’t be misled, Herald, even at home the path will be full of challenges. You must prepare yourself, because what you face here will determine whether you’re ready for what comes next —Eryndor said, pointing toward the horizon of the forest.
